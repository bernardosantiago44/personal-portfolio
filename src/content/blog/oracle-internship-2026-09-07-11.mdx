This was a fun week outside my usual development work. I started two volunteering projects, got a new addition to my desk, and received enough feedback on a refactoring merge request to keep me busy for a while.

## Learning to be on the other side of the interview

The first volunteering project involves mock interviews with students from other countries. I’ll meet students from different undergraduate programs, help them practice, and give feedback they can use for their next attempt.

I joined because I’ve been on the other side of the screen and know how valuable outside feedback can be. It’s also a chance to get more comfortable talking to strangers over Zoom. How exciting.

## Starting the mentoring program

The second project is a two-month Oracle mentoring program for university students in Tamaulipas. Volunteers are assigned two mentees, usually students in technology programs who are looking for advice on their first job.

This program is broader and more personal than a mock interview. It’s about sharing experiences and helping students work through questions about their professional lives.

I know I don’t have the experience of a senior engineer, but I’m excited to contribute what I can and learn from my mentees too. I have two meetings scheduled for next Monday, so I’ll write about them once we’ve met.

I should probably post about it on LinkedIn. Finding the motivation to post on social media is another matter.

## A refactor with a lot of feedback

Work was a little messier. I received feedback on my first major refactoring merge request, and I think the feedback was longer than the request itself.

I don’t feel bad about it, though. In a one-to-one conversation, my manager explained that the legacy system I’d been working on was more than 14 years old. He told me not to stress and suggested a better approach than the one I’d come up with.

After a week navigating what felt like a maze and another reworking my earlier changes, I’ve learned a lot about that part of the system. At least I now have a better idea of what not to do next time.

There’s still work left for next week. I know what needs to change; I just need to figure out how to make it happen.

## A new Lego car

I also added a Lego car to my desk. It took about four days to build, which was extremely satisfying. And it was on sale, so clearly that helped justify it.

Everyone thought it was cool. Now the other side of the desk looks a little empty, and I’m considering getting another one after the next paycheck. I’ll probably do it anyway.
