This week, I started my first full sprint with the CrowdTwist team. I’d already worked on tickets in the previous sprint, mostly UI changes and items carried over from earlier sprints, but working alongside ongoing product changes feels different. There’s a lot to learn, and I’m starting to get a better sense of how the team works.

## Getting to know CrowdTwist

I’m working on CrowdTwist, part of Oracle’s Unified Loyalty line of business within Consumer Industries. It gives businesses a central place to manage personalized rewards, campaigns, and customer engagement based on how people shop and spend.

The idea is familiar if you’ve ever used a loyalty program: what you buy, how much you spend, and how you pay can all shape the rewards you receive. Working on the system behind that has made me realize how many features and processes sit underneath an experience that looks simple from the outside.

Our sprints run for three weeks. I’m still getting used to the product and its codebase, so even a small ticket can involve more context than I initially expect.

## Finding my place on a remote team

My team has been warm and welcoming. There are ten of us: seven full-time team members, a manager, and two interns. Everyone has been supportive and flexible, and I feel like we’ve already made some good connections.

Most of the team works remotely from New York or California. That sometimes makes scheduling tricky, with almost three hours between some team members. I do wish I could meet them in person more often.

There are also two people from other CrowdTwist teams here in the office. We don’t work on the same things, but it’s nice to have people nearby who can help when something comes up.

## Tickets and backpacks

My goal was to have both assigned tickets ready for QA by the end of the week. I didn’t quite get there, but they’re almost done, and they’re scheduled for release next Thursday.

Outside development, I’ve also been helping with a volunteering project to donate backpacks and school supplies to children from kindergarten through high school. It’s taken quite a bit of coordination, but I know the effort is worth it.

We’ll be welcoming the children next week for workshops and handing out their backpacks. I’m looking forward to seeing it all come together, and I’ll write more about it in the next entry.
