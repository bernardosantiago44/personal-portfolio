We started a new sprint on Monday, along with our usual feedback meeting. We talked about our results, what went well, what didn’t go as planned, and what we could improve.

I only had one ticket officially closed during the sprint, but two more were ready for release. That feels like three pieces of work finished, even if the release queue hasn’t quite caught up yet.

## Getting more comfortable with the product

I’m starting to understand more of what comes up in meetings. I can connect the discussions to parts of the product and navigate the codebase with a little more confidence.

There are still areas I haven’t worked on. I know they exist and have some idea of what they do, but I don’t yet understand how they work.

Compared with a month ago, though, I feel more independent. I still ask for help, and the team is supportive when I do. That feels like good progress.

## Trying two new volunteering programs

I also signed up for two more volunteering programs. They’re remote, which isn’t my favorite format, but it does make scheduling easier.

The first is a mentoring program where Oracle employees support university students studying technology. We’ll share our experiences, answer questions, and hopefully give them some of the confidence people gave us when we were starting out.

The second involves conducting mock interviews for university students. I’ve never been the interviewer before, so this will be new for me. I can ask people in the office for advice, including the person who interviewed me, and we’ll have an orientation session too.

I’m looking forward to learning something different and meeting new people through both programs.

## An early Thursday release

By Thursday, one of my two queued tickets had been deployed. Getting there was a bit of an adventure.

We’d originally planned for Thursday, then tried moving it to Wednesday because my manager had several releases to handle for another intern who was leaving on Friday. That didn’t work out, so we ended up starting at 7 a.m. on Thursday.

The early start wasn’t too bad. It meant I could finish earlier, enjoy the afternoon, and hopefully avoid some traffic on the way home.

I’m making progress. The learning curve feels less steep than it did at the beginning, even if I still have a long way to go. I’m not stressed about that right now; I’m enjoying the internship and getting more comfortable with it.
