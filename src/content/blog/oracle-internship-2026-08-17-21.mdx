This was a busy week. On one side, I was helping coordinate a volunteering project to donate 85 backpacks filled with school supplies to children living in care homes. On the development side, I made my first release with the team.

Both involved more coordination than I expected, and both gave me plenty to learn.

## Bringing the backpack initiative together

This was the first volunteering project I’d participated in, and also the first one I’d helped coordinate from the beginning.

With support from other interns, we organized the purchase of 21 backpacks through contributions from 35 people in Consumer Industries and other departments. That was our part of the larger effort to provide 85 backpacks for children from kindergarten through high school.

Buying together gave us a 15% wholesale discount. It also made it easier for people to join: we handled the purchasing and logistics, so volunteers could contribute money without having to organize everything themselves. More people wanted to participate, which helped us move toward the goal.

It was a lot of work, but seeing everyone get involved made it worthwhile.

## My first release

The first ticket I ever worked on had already been approved, but busy schedules meant I only got to publish the changes last Thursday.

It was a small front-end change, but deploying it was a whole process involving both me and my manager. We spent about three hours on the release. Everything went smoothly, and it was a useful way to understand what happens after the code is ready.

I’m glad I got to experience that process early in the internship.

## Understanding the scope before writing the code

I also worked on a more complex ticket this week. It turned out to involve more modules than I’d originally understood, something I only fully realized after making changes.

I still haven’t grasped the full scope of one of those modules, so I’ll need to ask teammates for help understanding how the legacy code works. For future tickets, I want to have those conversations before I start coding.

I wouldn’t call the time wasted, because I learned a lot. But understanding the scope earlier would have made the work faster.

I don’t feel too bad about it. From conversations with other interns and former interns, some hadn’t made a deployment at this point yet. I think I’m learning at a good pace, even if the path isn’t always straightforward.
