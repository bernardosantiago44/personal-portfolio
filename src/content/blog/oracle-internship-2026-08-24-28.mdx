This week started pretty normally. On Monday, I went through my tickets as usual. Tuesday turned out differently: I’d planned to join a painting activity with children visiting from Casa Down Guadalajara, but the team had several tickets ready and needed an improvised release.

I was disappointed to miss the volunteering event, but the release gave me another chance to learn. It was only my second one, and I got to explore parts of the process I hadn’t worked through before.

## Asking how I’m doing

For the past two weeks, I’ve been working on a task that sits somewhere in the middle: not extremely difficult, but definitely not trivial. Being new to the product makes it harder to judge how long something should take.

I’m used to environments where I know exactly what needs to be done and by when. Here, I’m still learning the system while trying to make changes to it. That had started to make me wonder whether I was progressing too slowly.

I reached out to my manager and asked for his opinion. He told me that this was normal and somewhat expected, and that I was making good progress overall.

That conversation was a relief. It helped me feel motivated to keep going instead of measuring every unfamiliar task against work I already knew how to do.

## Getting feedback when everyone is remote

Most of my team works remotely from the US. I don’t have someone physically nearby whose expressions or body language might tell me how things are going.

Text and group messages are useful, but they don’t always give me much feedback on my progress. Asking directly helped more than trying to guess from a chat conversation.

## Becoming a little more independent

Overall, it was a productive week. I feel like I gave it my best effort, and my teammates and manager are noticing it.

I still make mistakes, but I’m getting better at solving some of them myself. And when I need help, I know I can reach out to people on the team or here in the office.

There’s still a lot to learn about the product and the company. For now, I’m happy that things feel a little more familiar each week. The encouragement from the people around me has made a big difference.
